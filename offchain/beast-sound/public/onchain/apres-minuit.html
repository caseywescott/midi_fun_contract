Après Minuit

Your melody
One note per token: name, octave and length, like E4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional. Switch cues to hear the same tune harmonized with Italian or French moves.

How it works. Each phrase is built from harmonic moves, short chord idioms chosen by weight with Koji's LCG: the Italian ones (the minor line cliché, the lament bass, the Andalusian descent, chromatic mediants, the Neapolitan, Phrygian and deceptive cadences, the Picardy third) and the French ones (ii–V–I, the cycle of fifths, secondary dominants, the tritone substitute, passing diminished chords, the backdoor ♭VII7, sus dominants, Lai's bossa turns, parallel major sevenths). The phrase ends on a cadence move: a half cadence for the question, a full one for the answer. In AABA and the other song forms, a returning A keeps its chords and tune with a new ending, and the bridge starts away from home. Every slider mixes the two schools, so French chords can colour an Italian cue and the other way round.

Takes. New take redraws the cue within its ranges: key and mode, tempo, form and phrase length, the chord mix (every idiom's weight moves, and a guest idiom from the other school can sit in), the melody's rhythm, contour and register, the comping, bass and drum patterns, the drum fills, the arrangement arc and the instruments. Variation sets how far it strays. New seeds keeps every setting and redraws only the notes.

The melody is Koji's bounded walk, landing on chord tones on strong beats and bending to each chord's altered notes. Sequence repeats a bar's shape on the next chord's root, which is how Legrand's melodies spiral down the cycle of fifths; Motif reuse brings phrases back over new chords, as Morricone does. In Harmonize mode, every chord path through your tune is scored against the enabled moves and the best one is kept, so the same melody can be heard in either vocabulary.
