Beasts V3 · Starknet · onchain MIDI

Beast Sound Atlas

Every Loot Survivor Beast composes its own piece of music inside a Starknet contract. Nothing is recorded: the contract turns the Beast's traits into a canon, writes it as a MIDI file and hands it to a chip synthesizer in the browser. This page builds that piece from the ground up, from a single seed to a four-voice canon, and every example below plays the exact notes the code produces.

Press Play on any piano roll; it loops until you press Stop. Each colour is one voice; the grey ticks at the bottom are drums. The examples use the same TinySynth engine and chip instruments the onchain player uses.

01 · Seeds

Everything starts from a number

A Beast's music has to come out the same every time anyone asks, on any node, for as long as the chain exists. So there is no randomness in the usual sense. Each Beast's identity (its species and its two-part special name) is hashed with Poseidon, the hash Starknet uses natively[13], into a sound seed, and further hashes of that seed give separate seeds for the motif, the canon, the orchestration and the ornaments. The same Beast always gets the same seeds, and two Beasts almost never share one.

Some decisions need many small random numbers in a row. For those the composer uses a linear congruential generator, the oldest trick in pseudo-random numbers[10][11]: multiply, add, keep the remainder. The countersubject in chapter 5 is written this way, with x → (5x + 3) mod 256. Because 3 shares no factor with 256 and 5 − 1 is a multiple of 4, this generator visits all 256 states before it repeats[12].

Drag the seed. The table shows the generator's states. Each state does two jobs: its last two bits (state mod 4) pick a step of −2, −1, +1 or +2 scale degrees, and the next two bits ((state ÷ 4) mod 4) pick the note's length, an eighth, a quarter, a dotted quarter or a half. One number gives both the pitch and the rhythm. The next chapters keep the pitches and give the rhythm to the Beast's rhythm cells instead.

02 · The motif

A melody is a walk through a scale

The theme is a bounded random walk in scale degrees. Starting on the tonic, each step moves one or two degrees up or down; a step that would leave the Beast's range (five degrees each way for low-register Beasts, seven otherwise) is turned around. Working in scale degrees rather than pitches keeps every note in the Beast's mode, and small steps give the theme the stepwise shape that species counterpoint asks of a cantus firmus[1].

In the real composer each step comes from a Poseidon hash of the motif seed, the position and the steps so far, so a theme cannot be predicted from a few of its notes. Themes are twelve notes long, sixteen for the two largest tiers, and the last bar is rewritten to step to the tonic and hold it: a cadence, so the theme sounds finished when it ends[8].

This walk uses the LCG from chapter 1 so you can follow it step by step. It is played in A Aeolian on a triangle lead.

03 · Rhythm

Four-beat cells give the theme a gait

Equal quarter notes sound mechanical, so the theme is cut into four-beat groups and each group takes a rhythm cell: plain quarters, a long note, a dotted figure, a run of eighths, a lilt, a syncopation, half notes. Which cells are available depends on the Beast's type, so the three families move differently: Magic flows, Hunters drive, Brutes are heavy. The last group is always the cadence cell. Where a cell adds a note between two beats, the new note is a passing or neighbour tone toward the next one, the standard way to fill a melodic gap[1][2]. A Beast's ornament style decides how many cells may carry these extra notes.

04 · Canon

One theme, several voices, staggered

A canon is the strictest form of imitation: a second voice sings the same melody as the first, starting later, and the two must sound good together at every moment[3][2]. Bach built whole cycles on it, with a canon at every interval in the Goldberg Variations[6]. When the entries come close together the effect is called stretto, a device fugues save for their climax[5].

A Beast's canon has one to four voices. Each follower enters a fixed number of beats after the one before (the stretto lag) and sits a set number of scale degrees away: by default a fifth below, a fourth above and a ninth below, closer or wider depending on the Beast. Followers can also sing the theme upside down (melodic inversion)[7]. A strict canon at these intervals would collide, so every note is checked against the voices sounding with it: thirds and sixths are preferred, and clashes on the beat, unisons and parallel fifths and octaves are avoided by nudging the note a step. That is the rulebook of species counterpoint[1], applied note by note, which makes this a free canon rather than a strict one[2].

The voice check here is simplified (unisons and fifths against earlier voices are nudged by one degree). The real composer also weighs beat position and parallel motion.

05 · Countersubject

A second melody that works above or below

Larger Beasts add a countersubject: a separate melody written against the theme, as fugues do[5]. It starts a third away and moves by small steps, and at every note it may only form a consonance with the theme (unison, third, fifth or sixth, counted in scale steps). It also avoids the fifth itself, because a fifth turns into a fourth when the voices swap places, and a fourth against the bass is a dissonance. Writing two lines so that either can be on top is called invertible or double counterpoint[3][4]. Which allowed note it takes at each step is chosen by the LCG from chapter 1, seeded by the Beast's name and species.

06 · Form and keys

Sections travel around the circle of fifths

A Beast's piece repeats its canon in two to five sections. Each section starts the theme on a different degree of the key, planned on the circle of fifths, the map of related keys that eighteenth-century theorists used to plan modulations[9]. Small, close Beasts move one fifth away and back; the largest tiers reach up to four fifths. The Beast's type picks the sharp or the flat side of the circle, and the last section always lands within a fifth of home so the loop returns smoothly.

Between sections nothing goes silent. Each voice plays an episode, a sequence of the theme's opening bar repeated a step higher or lower each time, a standard fugal link[5], and then a half cadence on the dominant of the next section's key, which makes the arrival feel inevitable[8].

07 · Ornament

Small figures decorate the line

On top of the structure sit ornaments, the short decorations Baroque players added to long notes: mordents, trills, turns[14]. In a Beast's theme, the second word of its special name picks an ornament style (whether it trills, whether it uses suspensions or chromatic approaches, and how dense the decoration is). Repeated notes in the inner voices are tied instead of struck again when they fall off the strong beats, which gives the texture the long held lines of fourth-species counterpoint[1].

One species gets more. The Yeti plays a rock groove at 175 BPM, ends each section with an "avalanche" (a run down the closing chord) and ornaments its theme in sixteenth notes. Where the figures fall is set by a hash of the Beast's name, so every named Yeti ornaments differently.

08 · Beast to music

Everything so far is the composer. What makes it a Beast's piece is the mapping from the Beast's traits to the composer's parameters. A Beasts V3 token packs the species, the two parts of the special name, level, health, the shiny and animated flags, the tier and the type into one 256-bit number, and the contract reads them straight out of the token ID.

10 · How it changes

Change without shuffling the parts

The first version of the composer (engine v1) let a Beast's current rank feed the structure: a top-ranked Beast gained a voice, a countersubject and a tighter stretto. The trouble is that rank moves every time another Beast's stats change. A shuffle in the leaderboard could add a player to the canon or take one away, so the piece was reorchestrated by events that had nothing to do with the Beast itself.

Engine v1.1 separates three layers. Identity (species, name, shiny) fixes the theme, key, instruments and form, and never changes. Current state (rank) sets only how prominent the lead is: its loudness and accents. Trajectory (the Beast's own history of kills and defeats) shapes how later sections develop the theme. The structure is always composed with a neutral rank, so a ranking shuffle can change the dynamics but never which voice plays which notes.

The Species Songs that go live at launch go one step further: they ignore rank, kills and defeats entirely, so every Warlock plays the same notes and nothing on the leaderboard can alter them.

The two pairs below are the same tier-2 Beast at rank 500 of 954 and at rank 1. Under v1 the top rank adds a fourth voice and tightens the canon (192 notes become 240). Under v1.1 the notes are identical and only the velocities differ.

Drift is seeded by block hashes rather than a plain day counter, so nobody can compute next week's version of a piece before the block that decides it exists. Block hashes are a public, if imperfect, randomness source[17]; here they only decide music, so no one gains much by influencing them. Each day's version is a pure function of the track, the day number and three block hashes, so any past day can be replayed exactly. The channel order uses a Fisher–Yates shuffle[15] driven by the week's hash.

11 · Onchain

From token to sound

All of the composition above runs inside a Starknet contract, written in Cairo. When a wallet, marketplace or website asks a Beast for its media, the call chain looks like this:

  1. The Beasts V3 NFT points each Beast at a SoundManager contract. The original 75 species point at BeastMidiProvider.
  2. BeastMidiProvider checks the token, decodes its traits from the token ID and calls the composer. It answers get_sound(token_id) with the MIDI file and the synth settings it is written for. It is upgradeable by its owner (OpenZeppelin Ownable and Upgradeable[20]) for a short period after launch, then the owner renounces and the class can never change.
  3. The composer library (beast_composer) is compiled into the provider: the trait mapping, the theme walk, rhythm cells, the canon with its voice checks, the countersubject, the key plan and episodes, the Genesis rules and the Yeti, and a Standard MIDI File writer[18]. The whole class has to fit Starknet's limit of 81,920 compiled words; it uses about 79,500.
  4. The synth settings are the exact chip instruments the MIDI selects (operators, waveforms and drum sounds), so the piece sounds the same on every player regardless of any preset bank.
  5. The sound page wraps the MIDI and settings into the token's metadata as a self-contained page, and the browser plays it with TinySynth[19], a small WebAudio synthesizer. Nothing is fetched from a server.

A JavaScript mirror of the composer runs the lab and this page. Scripts compare it with the Cairo code on every species, name and seed they test, and the two produce the same MIDI byte for byte, so anyone can check a Beast's music without trusting this page.
