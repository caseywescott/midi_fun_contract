Middle Eight

Your melody
One note per token: name, octave and length, like D4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional. Chords per bar follow the "Chord changes per bar" slider (over 60 gives two).

How it works

Chords from the records. The harmony is a statistical model of the Beatles' own chord changes. Every chord in the Isophonics transcriptions of the 180 songs on the twelve studio albums is written relative to its key (I, ♭VII, ii7, III7…). The page blends three periods' unigram, bigram and trigram counts by the Era sliders. Trigrams back off to bigrams where the corpus is thin. A phrase is not walked left to right in the hope that it lands well. It is sampled with Markov constraints: a backward pass first weighs every chord by how well it can still reach the phrase's ending. The first chord then follows how that kind of section opens in the corpus, and the last two follow how it ends: a tonic for an answer, a half cadence for a question, V at the end of a middle eight. Every chord in between is as likely as the corpus makes it, given those ends. An answer phrase keeps the question's chords and resamples only its ending, and a two-bar loop is sampled so that it wraps round. Chord lengths follow the period: mostly whole bars early on, twice as many two-beat chords later.

Named devices then rewrite some moves on purpose, and the lane labels each one. The corpus already contains them, but at their natural rate they can be hard to hear. Each slider makes one more likely: a line falling under the tonic, IV turning minor, the major II, Mixolydian ♭VII, the double plagal ♭VII–IV–I, augmented and diminished passing chords, III7 into vi, I turning to i, and a middle eight on ♭III–♭VII–IV–I. Sections can change key: up a half step, down a whole step, to the parallel minor, or to IV through I7.

Melody from the corpus too. Each note of the lead is drawn from a product of distributions, the way David Temperley models melody. The interval from the last note follows the corpus's interval histogram, and the scale degree follows its degree profile. A range window and a contour then rise and fall to the cadence. Strong and weak beats each get a chord-tone factor. In rock the tune often runs on its own pentatonic track over the chords, which Temperley calls the melodic–harmonic divorce. The independence slider sets how far that goes; at 50 it matches the Beatles' 67% chord tones on the beat. Rhythms come from the corpus's onset grid, including the eighth pushed ahead of the bar. A hook brings back a bar's rhythm and shape, an answer phrase repeats the question and finds a new cadence, and repeated sections repeat their tune. The melodist profiles use each writer's own numbers: Lennon repeats notes more and stays narrower, and McCartney moves by step and syncopates more. The Harrison profile rests on a single song, so it is mixed with the whole sample.

Readouts. Bits of surprise is the average −log₂ probability of each chord change under the current blend: lower means more typical of the chosen period. Closest songs are the corpus songs whose chord-to-chord moves overlap most with yours. Melody vs corpus measures the generated tune the way the corpus was measured, with the chosen melodist's figures in brackets.

Field notes

  • The major II. "Eight Days a Week" sets its verse on I–II–IV–I (D–E–G–D). The II is a major chord on the second degree, borrowed from the dominant of V, and here it goes nowhere near V. Device "Major II". Style: Mersey beat, Jangle.
  • Mixolydian ♭VII. The verse of "A Hard Day's Night" runs I–IV–I–♭VII–I, and its middle eight moves to iii and vi. "Norwegian Wood" rocks I–♭VII–I under a 3/4 strum, then turns its middle eight to the parallel minor with a Dorian IV (i–IV). Device "Mixolydian ♭VII", middle eight key "parallel". Styles: Jangle, Folk rock in 3/4.
  • IV turns minor. "In My Life" runs I–V–vi–I7–IV–iv–I: I7 points to IV, and IV darkens to iv before home. "If I Fell" uses the same IV–iv. Device "IV turning minor". Style: Baroque piano.
  • Chromatic passing chords. "All My Loving" slides vi–♭VI+–I through an augmented chord (C♯m–C+–E). "If I Fell" walks iii–♭iii°–ii7–V. Devices "Augmented passing", "Passing diminished".
  • Into vi through its dominant. "Yesterday" moves I–vii–III7–vi (F–Em–A7–Dm): a ii–V into the relative minor. Device "Into vi through III7". Style: Ballad.
  • Line clichés. In "Michelle" the minor tonic's top note falls by semitones (i–VII+–i7–i6). In "Something" the line falls in the bass under C, Cmaj7 and C7 to F. "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" does the same in minor: Am, Am/G, Am/F♯, F. In "Dear Prudence" the line falls under I–I7–IVmaj7–iv. Device "Descending line". Style: Harrison.
  • I turns to i. The verse of "Penny Lane" goes I–i–viø7–♭VImaj7–V (B–Bm–G♯ø–Gmaj7–F♯), and its chorus sits a whole step lower. Device "I to i", chorus key "down a whole step". Style: Psychedelic.
  • Three keys. "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds" puts its verse in A, its pre-chorus in B♭ and its chorus in G. Keys "up a half step" and "down a whole step". Style: Psychedelic.
  • The flat side by fifths. The middle eight of "Here Comes the Sun" repeats ♭III–♭VII–IV–I (C–G–D–A), each chord a fourth up from the last. Device "Middle eight ♭III–♭VII–IV–I". Style: Harrison.
  • Double plagal. The long coda of "Hey Jude" repeats I–♭VII–IV–I. Biamonte calls ♭VII–IV–I a double-plagal progression, two plagal steps in a row. Ending "Fade on I–♭VII–IV–I". Style: Piano anthem.
  • Chorus first, sixth chord last. "She Loves You" opens on its chorus with vi–II–IV–I and ends on a G6 chord. Form "Chorus first", ending "Final added-sixth chord". Style: Mersey beat.
  • Plagal hymn. "Let It Be" turns I–V–vi–IV and I–V–IV–I. Late in the corpus, IV→I is the commonest move of all. Style: Piano anthem.

Progressions are from the Isophonics transcriptions (C4DM reference annotations; Harte et al., ISMIR 2005), except "Hey Jude" and "She Loves You", which are from the de Clercq–Temperley corpus.
