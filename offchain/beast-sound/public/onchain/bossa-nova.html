The language of the study

Stone Flower. Jobim recorded Stone Flower in 1970 for Creed Taylor's CTI label, with arrangements by Eumir Deodato. He played piano, electric piano and guitar himself, with Ron Carter on bass, Airto Moreira on percussion, Joe Farrell on soprano saxophone and Urbie Green on trombone. Next to the classic bossa nova songs, the album leans into modal grooves that sit on a vamp, the music of Brazil's northeast, a choro, and slow songs for strings. This page generates in those manners. Every tune is made from seeds, and none quotes Jobim.

Chords. Tonics carry a major seventh, a ninth, a 6/9 or a sharp eleventh. Dominants come as 9, 13, 7(♭9), altered or 9sus4. The Lydian dominant II7(♯11) does not go straight to V: it softens to ii7, then a tritone substitute ♭II7(♯11) slides down a semitone home. Secondary dominants chain round the circle (III7 VI7 II7 V7). Bass lines fall by semitone under a held tonic (Cmaj7 C7/B♭ Fmaj7/A Fm6/A♭ C/G), and in minor keys the line clichés down from the tonic (Am Am(maj7)/G♯ Am7/G Am6/F♯). Borrowed chords (iv6, ♭VImaj7, the backdoor ♭VII9 and the Neapolitan ♭IImaj7) add shade. The modal vamps are Dorian (im9 IV13), Mixolydian (I13 ♭VII/I) and Lydian dominant (I7♯11 II/I), where the dominant-quality chord is a tonic and does not have to resolve.

Melody. Strong beats and long notes sit on consonances, and in this style those include the tensions: the ninth, the sharp eleventh, the thirteenth and the major seventh. A repeated-note phrase stays on one pitch while the harmony changes under it, and when no chord can hold the note, it shifts by a semitone. A chromatic line falls a semitone with each chord. Phrases begin off the beat and anticipate the barline. A sequence moves a phrase with its chord root.

Form. The forms are AABA and ABAC choruses, a canção with a bridge, a vamp form (intro vamp, a modal A, a functional B, then an outro vamp that fades) and a choro rondo (A B A C A). Bridges change key directly, a half step up or to ♭III, ♭VI, II, IV or the relative key, and return through a ii–V or a tritone substitute. The guitar plays a bossa pattern and anticipates changes on the “and” of four. The Chord check readout tests every change. Harmonize mode searches the same vocabulary under the same rules.
