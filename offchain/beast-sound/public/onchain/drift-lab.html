Drift Lab

Three ways a Beast's song can change as blocks go by, all on its Species Song. A is what the contract does now (loothero's evolution). B is a mutation chain: every block may mutate the track, the mutations stack, and the song grows toward noise. C is Casey Drift, a calendar that varies the song by day, week and month without accumulating. The timeline shows how far each one moves the song from as written.

A · EvolutionConfig

Experimentsnot in the contract
The velocity range and ornament chance are multiplied by 1 + growth × milestones ÷ 8.
  • There are 8 milestones, at birth + first age × 1, 2, 4 … 128. At each one, every locked part rolls once to join, and stays once it has.
  • Every block, the notes (velocity and octave ornaments) and the tempo re-roll from that block's hash. Nothing else carries over.
  • After the 8th milestone nothing new can happen.
B · Mutation chainan idea, not in the contract
  • Every block has a 1 in N chance of a mutation. Here a hash chain stands in for the block hashes.
  • Each mutation is permanent and they stack: a part joins, more octave ornaments, wider dynamics, a tempo step, or more pitch noise.
  • The notes stay as they are between mutations.
  • Only the ceilings cap the growth, so given time the track turns to noise.
C · Casey Driftyour Cairo (song_smf_bytes)
  • Month (30 days): two months in three re-pick the rhythm cells.
  • Week: three weeks in four rotate which channels play, keeping the count, the theme and the low voice.
  • Day: turns one knob. The key plan turns the other way, the episode directions swap, or the tempo moves −4, −2, +2 or +4 BPM.

Each day's version is a pure function of the song and the day, so nothing accumulates: the song wanders around its written form, and the same day always sounds the same. The page assumes 43,200 blocks a day, as the lab does, and seeds each day from its first block's hash.
