Drift Lab

Casey Drift is how a Beast's tracks change day by day. Each month may bring new rhythms, each week a new arrangement, and each day turns one knob. Every layer is seeded by the hash of its own first block, so nobody can hear a day before its block exists. Nothing accumulates: a day's version depends only on the track and that day. This page plays exactly what the composer contract's compose_drift returns.

On a Species Song
  • Month (30 days): two months in three re-pick the rhythm cells.
  • Week: three weeks in four rotate which channels play, keeping the count, the theme and the low voice.
  • Day: turns one knob. The key plan turns the other way, the episode directions swap, or the tempo moves −4, −2, +2 or +4 BPM.

A Species Song's drift belongs to its species: all Beasts of a species hear the same day. A Beast's other tracks drift by its own special name.

On an edition track
  • Month: two months in three bring new melodies. Their rhythms and contour are drawn from the month's hash; the key, chords and tempo stay.
  • Week: three weeks in four put the accompaniment's chords in their first or second inversion.
  • Day: turns one knob. The A and B progressions swap, the A and B tunes swap, or the tempo moves −4, −2, +2 or +4 BPM.
