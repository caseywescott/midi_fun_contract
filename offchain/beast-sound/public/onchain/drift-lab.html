Drift Lab

Casey Drift is how a Beast's tracks change day by day. Each month may bring new rhythms, each week a new arrangement, and each day turns one knob. Every layer is seeded by the hash of its own first block, so nobody can hear a day before its block exists. Nothing accumulates: a day's version depends only on the track and that day. This page plays exactly what the composer contract's compose_drift returns.

On a Species Song
  • Month (30 days): two months in three re-pick the rhythm cells.
  • Week: three weeks in four rotate which channels play, keeping the count, the theme and the low voice, and draw the ornament density under a ceiling that rises with time: up to 3 of 7 for the first 180 days, 5 to day 360, then 7.
  • Day: turns one knob. The key plan turns the other way, the episode directions swap, or the tempo moves −4, −2, +2 or +4 BPM, played tenuto when slower and staccato when faster.

A Species Song's drift belongs to its species: all Beasts of a species hear the same day. A Beast's other tracks drift by its own special name.

On an edition track
  • Month: two months in three bring new melodies (drawn from the month's hash), a new accompaniment and percussion figure and another form; two of those months in three move the key to the dominant or subdominant, and one in three to the style's other mode. The chords and tempo stay.
    • three weeks in four swap one part in or out (descant, bass, pad or percussion; one drops out when all four play), put the accompaniment's chords in their first or second inversion, and turn the ornament to the next kind (upper grace, lower grace, slide, mordent), draw its density (up to 30% of long notes for the first 180 days, 45% to day 360, then 60%) and move the bass to its next rhythm.
  • Day: turns one knob. The A and B progressions swap, the A and B tunes swap, or the tempo moves −4, −2, +2 or +4 BPM and the phrasing flips (legato and detached).
