One note per token: name, octave and length, like E4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional. The tune is harmonized with the progressions the current style has turned on; switch styles to compare.
Dilla chordsTwelve J Dilla voicings transcribed from piano-tutorial screenshots, at their original pitches (red: the bass note). Click one to hear it. The Dilla shapes voicing plays them in your loop, moved to each chord's root, and the J Dilla style builds progressions from them.

How it works. A loop is a progression of 1 to 8 chords that cycles over 1 to 8 bars, repeated to fill the loop. The chords come from weighted progression idioms (i–♭VI, i–♭VI–♭VII, Phrygian i–♭II, harmonic-minor i–V, the drill i–♭VI–vii°, pluggnb ii–V turnarounds, the royal road, I–V–vi–IV and more), chosen with Koji's LCG (plus two-chord vamps, neo-soul turns and Lydian floats for plugg, trapsoul, jersey and cloud) and voiced with a held top note so the loop sits still. The melody is a short motif from Koji's bounded walk, built from trap rhythm cells (tresillo 3-3-2, dotted eighths, triplets), repeated across the loop and changed in its last slot by one of Koji's motif transforms: inversion, retrograde, an octave jump, a displacement, or a new answer. Bells answer it in the gaps.

The 808 follows the kick and the chord roots, jumps octaves on offbeats and glides into the next note; the guide drums play a trap groove (half-time kick and snare with hi-hat rolls in 32nds and triplets), a phonk groove with cowbells, the jersey club five-kick bounce or the reggaeton dembow, with a fill into the restart. New take redraws the style within its ranges: key and mode, tempo, loop length, the number of chords and their rhythm, the idiom mix, the motif, the patterns and the sounds. Save MIDI gives one loop as a zip (full loop, one file per layer, chord chart, settings); Save pack gives a folder of takes named by style, key and tempo.
