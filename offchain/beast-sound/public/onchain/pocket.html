Pocket Symphonies

Your melody
One note per token: name, octave and length, like D4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional. The chords come from the style's families, then the bass engine inverts them so the bass line moves.

How it works

Chords come from the mode plus weighted families (borrowed iv, ♭VI, ♭VII and ♭III, the major II, chromatic mediants), then the devices of the 1966–68 California sound rewrite them: a descending chromatic bass line under inverted chords, IV/V in place of V, ♭VII over the tonic, a diminished seventh passing from IV to I⁶₄, runs of major-seventh chords, ii–V pairs that slide into a nearby key and back, and the augmented line cliché (I, I+, I6, I7). A bass engine then chooses each chord's inversion so the bass line steps instead of jumping, and with Key ambiguity the tonic appears only as I⁶₄. That way no chord stands squarely on its root until the chorus.

A piece is made of sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, episodes, round), each in its own key, mode, tempo and texture. Key changes can cut abruptly, the way the sections of a modular record do, or arrive through the new key's V7 or its ii–V. The vocal stack sings close harmony under the lead, holds "ooh" chords, or punches "bah-bah" offbeats. The round ending layers one phrase in staggered entries over a two-chord vamp.

Field notes

  • Inversions and an unsettled key. In "God Only Knows" the verse sits on inverted chords (D/A, F♯m/A, E/B), and the tonic E only appears with its fifth in the bass, so the key wavers between A and E. A diminished chord passes between IV and I/5, and the chorus gains confidence by moving to root position. Bass engine, Key ambiguity, Passing dim7, Clear chorus. Style: Unsettled heaven. (Secrets of Songwriting; The Arts Fuse; Reverb)
  • A key change seven seconds in. "Wouldn't It Be Nice" opens with a harp-like arpeggio on I–vi–ii–V in A, then the vocal enters a major third away, in F. A dominant seventh of the new key replaces the expected chord. Intro "Arpeggio in another key", Pivot "Through the new key's V7". Style: Teenage symphony. (Wikipedia; Key Change Blog)
  • Ambiguous and sliding. "Don't Talk (Put Your Head on My Shoulder)" leans toward G♭ major, but its only G♭ chord is in second inversion. It has a half-diminished chord and a ii–V vamp that slides into another key and back. Slide ii–V, Key ambiguity, string ensemble. Style: Chromatic hush. (Wikipedia; Paste)
  • A pocket symphony. "Good Vibrations" was recorded in separate sections across months and studios, then cut together: verse, refrain, two episodic digressions, a returning refrain and a coda. The keys and modes shift abruptly, and a descending minor verse is set against an ascending refrain in its relative major. Form "Modular". Style: Pocket symphony. (A Pocket Symphony; ROLI; Project MUSE)
  • The bass is not the root. Brian Wilson wrote non-root, moving bass parts (the fifth as a pedal point in "Wouldn't It Be Nice"), and Carol Kaye played them on the Pet Sounds and Smile sessions. Bass "Melodic (Kaye)" and "Pedal on the fifth". (Guitar World; Carol Kaye)
  • Slash chords. Wilson uses slash chords for tension and ambiguity: a chord over a bass note that isn't its root, IV/V in place of V, ♭VII over the tonic. IV/V weight, cadence "ii7–IV/V–I". (Disc Makers)
  • The falling chromatic bass. The Left Banke's "Walk Away Renée" descends A – E/G♯ – Em/G – Bm/F♯ – F°, then A/E – D/F♯ – D/E, a baroque line under inversions, with harpsichord and strings. Descending line weight. Style: Baroque pop. (Positive Feedback; Hooktheory)
  • Major-seventh glide. The Association's "Never My Love" bridge moves through major-seventh chords (A♭maj7, D♭maj7, G♭maj7) with inversions, suspensions and borrowed chords. Maj7 chain, Sus chords. Style: Major-seventh glide. (Hooktheory)
  • Jazz chords in a pop song. Rod Argent brought jazz voicings and Bacharach-style sequences into the Zombies. "A Rose for Emily" moves through diminished and minor-seventh chords, resolves to an unexpected major chord, then changes key for the chorus. Style: Organ jazz-pop. (Tape Op; Wikipedia)
  • Sunshine and stacked voices. Gary Usher and Curt Boettcher's Sagittarius (Present Tense, 1968), and Boettcher's the Millennium (Begin), are lush, layered vocal harmony over orchestral and baroque arrangements with psychedelic touches, and hardly any guitar. Vocals "Close harmony" with five voices, Phaser. Style: Sunshine choir. (Wikipedia: Present Tense; Wikipedia: Begin; Curt Boettcher)

The styles are studies of these idioms. Nothing here quotes a melody: every tune is generated from the seeds.
