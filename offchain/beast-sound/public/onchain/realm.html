Realm of Common Tones

Your melody
One note per token: name, octave and length, like D4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional. The harmony uses the style's chord families and the Mode chosen under Key and form.

How it works

The harmony walks a chord pool built from the mode plus the families you weight: chords borrowed from the parallel mode (iv, ♭VI, ♭VII, ♭III in major; IV, V, I in minor), the Lydian II, chromatic mediants (III, VI, ♭III, ♭VI as major triads), the hexatonic pole (♭vi in major: no common tones, the "magic" chord), the Phrygian ♭II and real dominants. Each step is weighted by root motion, by how many common tones the next chord shares with this one (Common-tone pull), and by how little the voices have to move (Smooth voice leading). Phrases end on the cadence formulas game composers lean on: ♭VI–♭VII–I, IV–iv–I, IV–♭VII–I, V–II–I, the Andalusian i–♭VI–♭VII–V, and others.

The melody puts chord tones on strong beats and steps between them in a scale that bends to each chord (over ♭VI in C, A and E become A♭ and E♭). When a chord changes under a melody note that both chords share, Hold common tones lets the melody sustain it across the change: the note stays put and the harmony moves under it. Gold rings in the roll mark those notes, and the bottom lane counts the common tones at every change (P, L and R keep two; chromatic mediants, CM, keep one; the hexatonic pole, H, keeps none).

Field notes

  • ♭VI–♭VII–I. Koji Kondo's signature cadence, first heard in Super Mario's level-clear jingle. ♭VII works as the dominant: it keeps scale degree 4 falling to 3. Styles: Overworld, Victory, Kingdom. (Nota Bene 8; Inquiry@Queen's)
  • Mixolydian drive. Ocarina of Time's Hyrule Field repeats I–♭VII–I, then runs I–♭VII7–I–♭VII7–I–♭VI–v–♭II7–V7–I: the ♭VII avoids a full cadence so the music keeps moving. Cadence "Kondo long". Style: Open field. (USU Theory IV)
  • Planed 6th chords. A Link to the Past's "Beginning of the Journey" steps IV–iii–ii–I as parallel 6th chords, with two arpeggios that only make the harmony together. Cadence "Planed". Planing weight. (Game Music Analysis Club)
  • The tonic, avoided. Zelda's Lullaby lingers on the dominant, moves V→IV (a retrogression) and ends on an unresolved V7; its melody is a five-tone cell. Tonic avoidance, Retrogressions, open cadence "IV–V7". Style: Royal lullaby. (Ludomusicology; five-tone melody)
  • Three ocarina notes. Saria's Song is built on F, A and B: F Lydian, or IV of C. The ocarina songs are three-note cells stated twice and then extended. Pitch set "Ocarina", Form "Ocarina song". Style: Forest dance. (Lost Woods in Lydian)
  • Dorian or Aeolian? Song of Storms (D minor, 3/4) alternates the major and minor sixth: an E minor chord (Dorian B♮) and a borrowed B♭. Style: Windmill waltz. (Thinkspace; Hooktheory)
  • ♭VI–♭VII as a key change. Minuet of Forest reaches its new key through major triads on ♭6 and ♭7 instead of a perfect cadence. Form "Mediant shift" moves the whole theme by a chromatic mediant over a held common tone. (Thinkspace)
  • Andalusian, bent. Gerudo Valley runs i–♭VI–♭VII7–V7, a variant of the Andalusian cadence, with harmonic-minor and Phrygian-dominant colour. Cadence "Andalusian". Style: Desert valley. (Zelda Dungeon)
  • Waltz with low arpeggios. Kakariko Village is a waltz on I, IV and V, laid out by low string arpeggios. Great Fairy's Fountain rides harp arpeggios through minor i, iv and v with major-seventh colour. Styles: Village, Fairy fountain. (Hooktheory: Kakariko; Hooktheory: Fairy)
  • Crystal arpeggios. Final Fantasy's Prelude arpeggiates I(add9) and vi(add9), then climbs IV–V–♭VI–♭VII. Texture "Prelude", Form "Prelude". (Hooktheory; The Sound Architect)
  • The royal road. IV–V–iii–vi (ōdō shinkō), the JRPG and J-pop ballad loop. Royal road weight. Style: JRPG ballad.
  • Blank space. Breath of the Wild's field music is solo piano playing scattered fragments, Debussy-like and pentatonic. Tears of the Kingdom keeps the piano and adds string pads. Texture "Sparse piano", Rests, Pad. Style: Ruins and sky. (Wu, TotK analysis; TheGamer)
  • Hearth and home. Howard Shore's Shire theme is anhemitonic pentatonic on tin whistle, over bodhrán, harp and dulcimer. Pitch set "Pentatonic", Drums "Bodhrán". Style: Village. (1000 Libraries)
  • Celesta magic. Hedwig's Theme: celesta, E minor slipping briefly into D♯ minor, every chromatic note, augmented sixths, chromatic mediants and planing. Chromatic neighbours, Mediants, Hexatonic pole. Style: Wizard's waltz. (Wikipedia)
  • Wonder. Frank Lehman's Hollywood Harmony: successions of non-functional, non-diatonic triads, chromatic mediants and the hexatonic pole are film music's main tool for wonder. Neo-Riemannian P, L and R keep two common tones and move one voice by step. (Lehman, OUP; Open Music Theory)
  • Chant and ostinato. Jeremy Soule's Skyrim builds on ostinatos, two-chord loops and plagal motion under a male chant. Form "Loop", Voicing "Open fifths", Drums "Taiko". Style: Northern epic. (USU Theory IV)

The styles are studies of these idioms. Nothing here quotes a melody: every tune is generated from the seeds.
