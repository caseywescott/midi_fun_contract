Kingdom of Sweets

Your melody
One note per token: name, octave and length, like E4/q, F#5/e., Bb3/h. Lengths: w h q e s, a dot adds half. A token without a length repeats the last one; r is a rest; | bar lines are optional.

How it works. The harmony is a functional walk (tonic, predominant, dominant) through Koji's progression grammar, then Romantic rewrites applied by weight: an applied dominant before its target, a diminished seventh in place of a dominant, an augmented sixth or Neapolitan before the cadence, borrowed iv and ♭VI, the cadential six-four, passing diminished chords. In Harmonize mode, every possible chord path through your melody is scored (melody notes as chord tones, functional flow, each chromatic chord resolving where it must) and the best one is kept.

Forms and devices. A piece is built from sections, each with its own key and texture. Ternary puts a trio in another key (relative, mediant, or a chromatic mediant) between an A and its decorated return, reached over a held common tone or through a diminished-seventh or German-sixth pivot, with a retransition on a dominant pedal. Waltz chain opens with a harp cadenza and strings waltzes in different keys. Glinka variations keep the tune and change everything around it: instrument, countermelody, harmony, register, density. Grand adagio states the descending scale theme, more chromatic each time, and augments it at the climax. Phrases can be expanded by a deceptive cadence; phrases can open on a chromatic lament bass or a descending-fifths sequence; held-over voices make suspensions; a common-tone diminished seventh or augmented sixth shimmers over a tonic that stays put. The Russian settings add ♭VII and plagal closes, mutable cadences in the relative key, and a magic episode on Glinka's whole-tone scale or the octatonic scale.

The countermelody is Koji's countersubject logic made harmony-aware: chord tones on strong beats, thirds and sixths against the melody preferred, contrary motion rewarded, moving while the melody holds, and parallel fifths and octaves forbidden. The bottom lane shows its motion on each beat: C contrary, O oblique, S similar, and a red mark if a parallel fifth or octave ever slips through.
