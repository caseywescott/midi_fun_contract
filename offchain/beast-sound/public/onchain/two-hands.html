Two Hands, Two Times

Cross-rhythms between the hands, generated with Koji's primitives: one hand divides the cycle one way, the other another way, over planed chords and pedal points.

How it is built. Both hands share one cycle (a beat, two beats or a bar). The right hand divides it into one number of equal pulses and the left hand into another, so their onsets only meet where the two grids coincide: every 1/gcd of the cycle. Euclidean thinning (Toussaint's even distribution of k onsets in n pulses) removes pulses without clumping them. Melodies are Koji's bounded walk in scale degrees, driven by a linear congruential generator; harmony moves by planing (chords sliding in parallel, either inside the scale or with their exact intervals kept, which leaves the scale) over optional pedal points.
