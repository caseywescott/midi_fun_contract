The language of the study

The record. On “A Journey to Reedham (7am Mix)” the melodic line starts out slow. As the track builds, it speeds up to about 1, then 3, then 18 to 30 note changes a second, at a little under 130 BPM. At the peak, the line is mostly broken-chord oscillation: a moving upper note against a held lower one, like C♯–F♯–C♯–F♯ or G♯–E–G♯. Between those come chromatic clusters (C–C♯–D) and two-octave arpeggio sweeps. Underneath, chords with a major seventh move by thirds and steps in G Lydian (Gmaj7 Bm7 A7 F♯m7 Cmaj7), as often as once a beat. The centre lifts toward D, where G♯ appears, and the track closes on a IV V ii iii I in D. Those measurements are where this page starts. Every piece is generated from seeds, and none quotes the record.

Motion in three layers. The engine follows Koji's melodic_motion split. First, a structural line moves one note a beat, and every beat lands on a chord tone or a safe tension. Next, the ornament density decides, beat by beat, whether to divide the beat and into how many parts. The odds are your subdivision sliders, scaled by the flow, so peaks lean toward sextuplets and thirty-seconds. Last, a fill figure connects one structural note to the next. The figures are a scale run, a chord-tone sweep, a compound melody that alternates with a pedal (compound_melody), a chromatic slide (ORN_FILL_CHROMATIC), a turn or trill (ornamentation_v2), or the motif itself compressed into the beat. Stream lock keeps a subdivision and figure going for many beats, which gives the long motoric runs.

Motif. A seeded cell of three to eight scale steps is developed phrase by phrase with Koji's motif_algebra. Each phrase applies one operation: statement, sequence, fragment, stutter, inversion, retrograde, retrograde inversion or expansion. As in motif_form, early phrases favour statement and sequence, and later and contrasting sections favour inversion and retrograde. Sections with the same letter reuse their material, so the recurring section is heard again with fresh ornaments over its own chords.

Harmony. Chords are built in thirds from the chosen mode, or as triads, ninths, fourths or parallel major ninths. They move by the parsimonious rule of parsimonious_progression: root motion by third and step is preferred, chords that share tones are preferred, and each section ends on a cadence into the next centre. The flow sets the harmonic rhythm: two bars a chord when quiet, one bar, half a bar, then one a beat at the peak. Centre drift lifts the peak sections, a fifth up by default, as the record does from G toward D. Chord voices are placed by the least total motion between chords (voice_leading).

Canon. Each follower plays the lead at an interval in the mode: a fifth, an octave, a third, a fourth or an echo at the unison. It enters a fixed lag later, can be inverted, and is realised in the scale of the moment it sounds. Stretto (stretto, entry_lag_canon) draws the entries closer as the flow rises, down to an eighth of a beat. With Voices enter with flow on, quiet bars thin to the lead. Beat voice checks (canon_rules) move any follower note that lands on a beat outside the chord, or a semitone from another voice, to the nearest safe tone.

Region takes. Drag across the flow or harmony lane to choose bars, or drag a box on the piano roll to choose the voices inside it. Then press New take. Only the selected voices inside the selected bars are regenerated, with a fresh seed and, if you like, their own mode, motion or figure. The line enters the region from the note before it and heads for the note after it. Everything outside is copied back unchanged. Canon followers of a retaken lead change one entry lag later, and nothing else moves. Pressing New take again on the same region tries another take. Each take is listed, and you can remove it or undo it.
